Horror Games Without Gore: How to Check Content

To find a horror game without gore, look for a specific content note about blood, injury, and graphic imagery rather than assuming “psychological horror” means clean visuals. Check the story themes separately: a game can show no gore and still deal with a missing person or other upsetting ideas.

Read the content words precisely

The ESRB ratings guide distinguishes “Blood” from “Blood and Gore” and defines the latter in terms of blood or mutilation. Those descriptors can help when a game has an ESRB rating, but a broad age rating alone is not a detailed content note. For an unrated indie game, read the maker's own warning and look at representative screenshots.

A practical content check

  1. Write down what you want to avoid: graphic injury, visible blood, body horror, violence, or disturbing themes. They are different checks.
  2. Read the game's official content note and, if available, its rating descriptors. Look for precise terms rather than treating the genre name as a warning.
  3. Review a few official screenshots or a free opening. Stop if the material already crosses your line; remember that an opening cannot show every later scene.
  4. Search the game's name with the specific concern, such as “gore” or “blood,” and check a source that discusses the edition you intend to play.
  5. If the maker gives no clear answer, ask the maker for a spoiler-light content note before buying or playing.

A useful question to send is: “Does the full game show visible blood, graphic injury, or body horror? I am happy with suspense, but I want to avoid those images.”

What the apartment mystery contains

The Apartment Calls Back states “Contains: suspense, a missing person. No gore.” The story appears as written calls and records, without graphic injury or gore imagery. The free opening begins when you press Answer the call and mentions a friend who has stopped replying; it lets you check the tone before continuing.

The full edition asks you to read three recorded calls, select two conflicting items, and press Compare. Its possible endings keep the missing-person tension. No-gore here is a content statement, not a claim that the story is suitable for every age or sensitivity.

FAQ

Does psychological horror mean there is no gore?

No. It describes the source of tension, not a guarantee about images or text. Check the specific game's content note and screenshots.

Does The Apartment Calls Back show blood or graphic injury?

No. Its content note says “No gore,” and the game uses written calls and records without graphic injury imagery. It does include suspense and a missing person.

Can a game without gore still be frightening?

Yes. Uncertainty, a threatening voice, or an unresolved disappearance can be unsettling without graphic bodily detail. Decide whether those themes fit what you want to play.

Is a free opening enough to rule out gore later?

No. A preview only shows its own section of a game. Read the maker's full-game content note and look for information about later scenes before you rely on a no-gore claim.
