Horror games without audio: what to check

Choose a horror game that puts every clue and instruction on screen, with no puzzle that depends on hearing a sound. Mute your device during the opening and check that you can still follow the story and make each choice.

Try the text-only opening

Check more than the mute button

A game may let you turn its volume off and still expect you to hear footsteps, a spoken code, or a warning cue. Use this checklist before deciding whether it works for a silent session.

CheckWorks without audioNeeds a closer look
DialogueEvery spoken line is written out.Voices carry facts that captions omit.
CluesRecords, labels, or text show what matters.A sound pattern is the only evidence.
ActionsVisible controls tell you when to continue or choose.A tone alone tells you when to act.

A description saying “subtitles” is useful, but it does not by itself prove that every gameplay cue is visible.

A quick mute test

  1. Mute your device before opening a game or its free preview.
  2. Read the first scene. Check whether spoken lines, caller names, and instructions appear as text.
  3. Try the first interaction. Ask whether you knew what to do from the screen alone.
  4. If the game has a puzzle, check whether it shows its evidence visually. If a sound is essential and has no written equivalent, this game may not fit your needs.

This test checks the opening, not every later scene. Look for a clear statement from the maker about audio-dependent mechanics before committing to a longer game.

A text-only phone mystery you can check

In The Apartment Calls Back, the phone calls are written transcripts, not sound recordings. The free opening starts with the on-screen line “Caller ID: HOME — APT 4B.” Press Answer the call, then Continue to read it line by line. There is no microphone or voice input.

The full game shows a Call log — Apt 4B. Each call has written record details and written dialogue; select two items and press Compare to test a contradiction. The Hint button is optional. The free opening ends before that puzzle, so use it to check the reading layout and tone; the full game contains the comparison activity.

Expect suspense and a missing person. There is no gore in the game text, but a quiet game can still feel unsettling.

FAQ

Can a phone horror game work with the sound off?

Yes, if its calls and clues are written on screen and no action depends on a sound cue. In The Apartment Calls Back, calls appear as text and the puzzle uses selectable written items.

Do I need headphones for The Apartment Calls Back?

No. The game has no audio track to listen to, and its phone calls are presented as text. You can read the free opening without sound.

Are the three recordings actual audio files?

No. “Recording” is the game's label for the written call transcript. The full edition lets you compare that text with written call-record details.

Does playing without audio make the story less tense?

That is a personal reaction. This game builds its suspense through dialogue, the call records, and the decisions you make, so muting your device does not remove a required clue.
