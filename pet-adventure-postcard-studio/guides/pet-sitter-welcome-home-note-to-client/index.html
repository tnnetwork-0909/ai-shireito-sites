Pet sitter welcome home note to client: what to write

Thank the client for trusting you with their pet, mention one moment you actually observed, and welcome them home in your own words. Send the final feeding, medication, access, and handoff details through your agreed care channel so a keepsake note does not have to carry urgent information.

Write the note

A note you can adapt

Replace each bracket with a true detail from this booking. Leave out any sentence that you cannot confirm.

First booking: “Welcome home, [client name]. Thank you for trusting me with [pet name]. During our time together, [one action you personally saw]. I enjoyed caring for [pet name]. — [your name]”

Repeat client: “Welcome back! I appreciated seeing [pet name] again. This time, [one real moment from the stay]. Thank you for inviting me to care for [pet name]. — [your name]”

If you had no suitable photo, a handwritten note or plain text works. Pet Sitters International includes a thank-you note after a first booking among possible personal touches; it does not make one a requirement of pet care. Read its suggestions for client communication.

Finish the booking without leaving a gap

  1. Confirm the final care facts. Check your notes against the last visit: what was done, what remains, and whether anything needs the owner's attention.
  2. Send the handoff in the agreed channel. State any time-sensitive fact directly. Confirm the return or next-care arrangement according to your agreement; do not assume that a welcome note transfers responsibility.
  3. Write the optional welcome note. Use the short wording above and one observed detail. Avoid guessing the pet's feelings or writing a story in the pet's voice as if it happened.
  4. Check the delivery. If leaving a paper card, put it where the client agreed to find it. If sending a picture, use their private channel and check that the frame has no address, keys, or personal documents.

FAQ

What should a pet sitter write in a welcome home note?

Thank the client for trusting you, name their pet, and add one detail you personally observed. Close with a simple welcome home or sign-off. Keep care instructions in the final handoff message.

Can I write the note from the pet's point of view?

You can choose a playful tone, but do not invent a pet's thoughts or an event that did not happen. A first-person note from you with a real observation is easier to keep accurate. The postcard app prints your words rather than creating a story.

Should I leave a photo card after every pet sit?

No. A card is optional and depends on what the client likes and whether you have a suitable, permitted photo. A timely, accurate care handoff matters even when there is no card.

Can a welcome home card replace my last visit update?

No. A keepsake line cannot reliably carry every care fact, concern, or next step. Send those details through the channel you agreed with the owner and make sure they know when care is handed back.
