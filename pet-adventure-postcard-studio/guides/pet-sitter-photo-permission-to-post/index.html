Can pet sitters post client pet photos on social media?

Ask the owner for clear permission before posting a pet photo publicly; permission to send a private visit update does not cover a public post. Even with permission, review the whole image and caption for names, addresses, home details, and clues that the household is away.

Pet Sitters International recommends asking clients before posting and avoiding names, addresses, or images that identify a home. Rover's community guidelines likewise tell providers to get permission before sharing pet photos outside private client communication. Follow any agreement or platform rules that apply to your booking.

Ask in plain language

Use a question that names the photo, the destination, and the choice. For example:

“May I share the photo of [pet name] from [visit/date] on my [named social account or website]? I would leave out your name and location. A no is completely fine; I can keep it in our private update only.”

For a future booking, record the owner's choice in the service agreement or your normal client record and check it before each public use. Pet Sitting NZ's code of conduct also distinguishes permission for a pet from permission to reveal a home or its security details.

Check a photo before sharing it

  1. Confirm the use. Read the client's answer for the specific public account or site. If the answer is unclear, ask again or keep the photo private.
  2. Inspect the entire frame. Look past the pet for an address, house number, mail, a collar tag, a screen, another person, or anything that identifies the household.
  3. Read the caption on its own. Remove the owner's name, location, travel dates, and any hint that the home is currently empty.
  4. Check the finished image. Preview the exact file you plan to share. A cropped card can still show private details inside the remaining frame.
  5. Use the right channel. Send a private update through the agreed client channel. Publish only if the separate permission and final image both pass your check.

FAQ

Does a client's permission for photo updates let me post publicly?

Do not assume it does. A private update to the owner and a public social post have different audiences. Ask specifically about public use and follow the client's choice.

What if the pet's face is the only thing in the picture?

Still check the background, collar, and caption for identifying clues, and obtain permission for public use. A close-up can reduce visible home details, but it does not grant permission by itself.

Can I share a postcard image if I made it on my own device?

Making a file locally does not decide where it may be shared. You can send it privately according to the client's preference; ask separately before using it in public marketing. Check the finished card because the exported image includes the photo and any text you typed.

What if the owner does not reply to my request?

Leave the image out of public posts until you have a clear answer. You can still send the agreed care update through the usual private channel. Do not delay an important care message while waiting for photo permission.
