Pet sitter photo updates to clients: what to send

Choose a clear photo from the actual visit and pair it with a short, specific account of what you observed. Send the care details the owner expects in your usual update; a photo or keepsake card can add context, but it should not hide a problem or replace important information.

Use the update checklist

A photo update you can send after a visit

  1. Check the client's preference. Use the channel and update schedule you agreed on. Keep a photo private to the client unless you have separate permission to publish it.
  2. Pick one clear moment. Choose a photo from this visit where the pet is easy to see and comfortable. Check the whole frame for an address, keys, documents, other people, or anything the client would not want shared.
  3. Describe what the image shows. Name an action you actually saw, such as resting, sniffing, or playing, if that happened. Do not infer the pet's mood from a pose or reuse an old photo as if it were current.
  4. Add the care facts separately. In your regular message, include the feeding, water, walk, litter, medication, or other details this owner asked you to report. If something needs attention, contact the owner directly through the agreed channel instead of relying on a cheerful picture.
  5. Review and send. Check that the name, date, photo, and words belong to the same visit. Send the photo and message through your normal client channel; keep any optional keepsake card distinct from the care update.

For more on photo selection and factual visit reporting, see Time To Pet's photo guide and its post-visit update guide.

Short caption prompts

Fill these in only with details from the current visit. They are prompts, not stories to copy as if they happened.

  • Activity: “[Pet's name] [action you saw] during [part of the visit].”
  • Quiet moment: “After [care task you completed], [pet's name] [visible action].”
  • Care update alongside a photo: “Photo from today's visit: [what the image shows]. [Requested care detail you can confirm].”

If the owner needs a complete visit report, write that message first. A photo caption is only a description of the image; it cannot confirm every care task on its own.

FAQ

What should a pet sitter include with a photo update?

Say what the photo actually shows and include the care details that client asked to receive. Keep uncertain impressions out of the message, and report a concern directly rather than letting the picture speak for itself.

Is one photo enough for a visit update?

One clear, current photo can illustrate a visit, but the right number depends on what you agreed with the owner. A photo does not replace a note about feeding, medication, or other requested tasks.

What if the pet would not pose for a photo?

Do not disrupt care or pressure the pet for a picture. Send an accurate text update and, if a photo was expected, explain that you did not get a usable one this time.

Can the postcard app send the update to the client?

No. It downloads a JPG or print PDF to your device; you decide whether and how to share the file. Use your usual client channel for the care update and any attached card.
