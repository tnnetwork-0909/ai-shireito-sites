Why is my 4x6 photo cropped?

A photo loses its edges when its shape does not match the space it must fill, or when a borderless printer expands the page. If the subject is cut off in the card preview, choose a photo with more room around it; if the card preview is complete but the print loses edges, inspect the print preview and printer settings.

Find where the crop happens

Find where the missing edge went

  1. Open the original photo. Check whether the pet's ears, paws, or another important detail are already outside the camera frame. A card or printer cannot restore an edge that was never photographed.
  2. Compare the card preview with the original. If the original contains the detail but the card does not, the photo is being cropped to fill the card's image area. A wide photo in a narrow frame can lose much of its left and right sides; a tall photo can lose some top or bottom.
  3. Compare the exported file with the card preview. If they match, the file contains the intended card layout. Inspect the entire file before changing print settings. The Pet Adventure Postcard Studio preview, JPG, and PDF use the same card image.
  4. Inspect the printer or print-service preview. If the file shows the whole card but the print preview trims its outside edge, confirm 4 × 6 inch paper, landscape orientation, and Actual size or 100% for a 6 × 4 inch PDF. Turn off borderless expansion if your printer offers that control, or use a print option that preserves the full page.
  5. Test one copy. Look at the photo and the card's outer margin separately. Change the photo if the subject is lost inside its frame; change print settings if the entire card is trimmed at the paper edge.

Photo-print providers explain that mismatched image and print proportions require a crop or a border. See FOREVER's explanation of photo aspect ratios and Modern Postcard's crop and border examples. Epson's print-setting guide also describes the slight crop caused by borderless expansion. The exact controls available to you depend on your printer or service.

Check the crop in Pet Adventure Postcard Studio

This app makes a landscape 6 × 4 inch card, but the visit photo fills a separate 3 × 3.5 inch area on the left. That photo area is portrait-shaped, while the whole card is landscape-shaped. The app centers the photo and crops it to fill the area; the three card styles do not offer a crop-position or fit control.

  1. On a computer, add a JPG, PNG, or WebP visit image using Photo (required). Use the original file when possible.
  2. Enter only what you observed in What you observed (required), then select a Card style.
  3. Look at Postcard preview, especially the pet's ears, paws, and any detail near the photo's edges. If the crop cuts something important, choose another shot with more space around the pet and inspect the preview again.
  4. When the preview is right, use Download print PDF for 4 × 6 inch paper or Download image (JPG) to share through your usual channel. For the PDF, follow the 4x6 PDF print steps.

A resolution warning is about possible softness, not where the crop sits. If no photo keeps the subject intact in this fixed layout, use a different layout that can show the full image. FAQ

Why does a 4x6 photo cut off part of my pet?

The printed image space may have a different width-to-height shape from your original. When a layout fills that space, it removes parts of the photo that fall outside the frame. Choose an original with more room around the pet and check the final preview.

Does a 4x6 photo avoid cropping on this postcard?

Not necessarily. The whole card is 6 × 4 inches, while its photo frame is 3 × 3.5 inches. Even a photo shaped for a full 4 × 6 print can be cropped inside that portrait-shaped frame.

Can I move the crop inside the postcard app?

No. The app uses a centered crop and does not include a drag or fit control. Try a different original with the pet nearer the center and more space at its edges, then check Postcard preview.

Why does the PDF look right but the printed card lose an edge?

That points to the printing step rather than the photo placed inside the card. Confirm the paper size and orientation, select Actual size or 100%, and inspect any borderless expansion setting. Make one test print before producing more copies.
