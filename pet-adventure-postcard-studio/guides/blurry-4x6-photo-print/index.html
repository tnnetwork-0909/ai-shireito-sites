Why is my 4x6 photo print blurry?

A small screen can hide missing detail, camera blur, and heavy cropping that become visible on paper. Check the original photo’s pixel dimensions and focus, then inspect how much of that photo fills the printed area; changing a file’s DPI label or enlarging a small image cannot recover detail that was never captured.

Check the photo

Work from the original photo

  1. Find the original file. Use the photo from the camera or phone, not a thumbnail, screenshot, or compressed copy from a message. Compare its pixel width and height with any copy you were about to print.
  2. Inspect the subject at a larger view. Zoom in on the pet’s eyes, fur, or other fine detail. If those areas were out of focus or blurred by movement when captured, more pixels or a different export format will not make them sharp.
  3. Check the crop. A photo that fills a frame may lose parts of its sides or top and bottom. Keep the pet away from the edges and examine the final crop before exporting.
  4. Check the pixels at the placed size. A full-page 6 × 4 inch photo would need 1800 × 1200 pixels for 300 pixels per inch. A smaller photo area needs fewer pixels; the exact requirement depends on its printed dimensions.
  5. Test the printer. If the source looks sharp and has enough pixels, try the correct photo-paper media setting and your printer’s higher-quality print setting. Microsoft’s print-quality guide also points to scaling, driver and printer issues as possible causes.

How the photo postcard app checks your image

In the app, one visit photo fills a centered 3 × 3.5 inch area on a landscape 6 × 4 inch card. For its 300 ppi target, the source photo needs at least 900 pixels wide and 1050 pixels high in its placed orientation. The app calculates an effective resolution from the photo’s pixel dimensions and shows a warning below its target; the warning does not block download.

On a computer, choose a JPG, PNG, or WebP photo in the Photo (required) field, then check Postcard preview before using Download print PDF or Download image (JPG). If the warning appears, use the original, uncropped photo when possible. The exported JPG is 1800 × 1200 pixels, but that output size cannot add real detail to a small source photo.

FAQ

How many pixels do I need for a 4x6 print?

At 300 pixels per inch, a photo covering an entire landscape 6 × 4 inch page needs 1800 × 1200 pixels. In this app the visit photo covers only 3 × 3.5 inches of the card, so its 300 ppi check uses 900 × 1050 pixels in the placed orientation.

Will changing DPI to 300 fix a blurry picture?

No. Changing a resolution label without adding real captured detail does not fix a low-pixel or out-of-focus original. Start with the largest, sharpest original photo you have.

Why does the app warn about my photo but still let me download?

The warning flags that the photo may print soft at the size used on the card; it is not a claim that the download failed. You can inspect the preview and decide whether to use that photo or choose a better original.

Can a sharp screen preview guarantee a sharp print?

No. A screen preview is usually viewed smaller than the physical print and may hide weak focus or low pixel count. Inspect the original at a larger view and make one test print if the result matters.
