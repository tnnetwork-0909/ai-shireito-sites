Gifts for pet sitting clients

Choose something that fits the family and what you know from caring for their pet: a handwritten note, a permitted pet photo keepsake, or a practical item they have said they would use. If you use a visit photo, describe only what happened and give the owner any care details through your usual update.

Before you give a photo keepsake

  1. Start with the client's preference. Use a photo only if the owner is comfortable receiving it. Permission to send a private update is separate from permission to post the image publicly.
  2. Choose one genuine moment. Pick a current, clear picture that shows the pet naturally. Crop out addresses, documents, keys, and other people where possible; choose another photo if the frame cannot be made private.
  3. Write a keepsake line about a real moment. For the card, try “A little memory from our visit: [pet's name] [specific action you witnessed].” Replace the brackets with something you can verify, or choose another moment you actually observed.
  4. Keep the thank-you separate. Add a handwritten or accompanying message such as “Thank you for trusting me with [pet's name].” Send feeding, medication, or concerns through the agreed care channel, where the owner will expect them.
  5. Check the final file or print. Confirm the name, photo, wording, and crop match the same pet and visit. If you print a card, inspect one copy before giving it away.

FAQ

Do pet sitters need to give clients a gift?

No. Accurate care and the updates you agreed to provide come first. A note or photo is an optional gesture if it suits your relationship with the client.

What can I write on a pet photo card for a client?

Use one visible or personally observed detail from that visit, such as the action in the photo, and check that the pet's name and date are right. Do not write from the pet's point of view if it would imply thoughts or events you cannot know. Put any important care report in your normal message.

Can I use a client pet's photo on social media too?

Do not assume that sharing a picture privately with the owner gives permission to publish it. Ask for separate approval before posting or using the photo in marketing, and leave out identifying details from the client's home.

When is a good time to give a pet photo card?

Give it after you have sent any expected care update, at a handoff or another moment that suits the client's preferences. If you do not meet in person, you can send the JPG privately through your usual client channel. Share any care concern directly rather than waiting to give the card.
