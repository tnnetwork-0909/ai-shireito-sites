When payment is still pending

How to ask a client for a payment date on an overdue invoice

If the invoice is still open, ask for a calendar date you can act on. This works even if you did not send an earlier reminder. Replace the bracketed fields and review the facts before sending.

Email example

Subject: Payment date for overdue invoice [invoice number]

Hi [client name],

Invoice [invoice number] for [amount and currency] was due on [due date] and remains unpaid.

Please reply with the date you expect to send payment. If something is preventing payment, tell me what needs to be resolved so we can agree on a next step.

Payment details: [link or instructions]

Thanks,
[your name]

Ask for a date, not a general update

“Any update?” can lead to “soon.” A request for the expected payment date gives the client a specific question to answer. Keep the original due date in the message, and use the unpaid balance if any partial payment has already arrived. Remove the payment-details line if the client has the correct instructions and it adds no value.

Use the answer to choose your next step

  • They give a date. Note the date in your own records and check whether payment arrives. If it passes, refer to that agreed date in your next message.
  • They say “soon.” Reply with one short question: “Could you give me the calendar date you expect to send it?”
  • They identify a blocker. Ask what document or correction they need. Resolve the invoice issue before escalating the tone.
  • They say they paid. Ask for the payment date or reference, then verify receipt through your own payment records.

Only set a reply deadline you can realistically follow up on. Avoid mentioning fees or consequences that are absent from your agreement. Keep a record of what the client actually promises.
