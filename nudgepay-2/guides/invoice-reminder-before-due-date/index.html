Before payment is due

How to remind a client about an invoice before the due date

Give the client a useful heads-up without suggesting they are late. Copy the example below, replace each bracketed field, and check the details against the invoice before sending.

Email example

Subject: Invoice [invoice number] due [due date]

Hi [client name],

Invoice [invoice number] for [amount and currency] is due on [due date]. I wanted to send a reminder in case you need time to arrange payment.

Payment details: [link or instructions]

Could you arrange payment to arrive by the due date? If you need another copy of the invoice or anything corrected, please let me know.

Thanks,
[your name]

Make the reminder useful

  1. Check that the invoice was delivered. A reminder is easiest to act on when the client already has the original invoice. Attach or link it again if they may have lost it.
  2. Use the date printed on the invoice. Write the full calendar date instead of “next Friday,” which can be read differently across time zones.
  3. Show the route to payment. Include the actual payment link or instructions, or say where they appear on the invoice. Delete the “Payment details” line if it adds nothing.
  4. Give them a way to fix a blocker. If the client needs a purchase order number or a corrected invoice, invite them to say so before the due date.

When to send it

A few days before the due date can leave room for an approval process. Check the client's working days and your agreement; a weekend may change when the message is useful. Do not call the invoice “overdue” while the due date is still ahead.

If the client has already confirmed a scheduled payment, you may not need another reminder. If the due date passes without payment, use a first overdue note that states the original date and asks for payment plainly.
