Follow-Up Email After a Missed Promised Payment Date

If a client’s promised payment date has passed, first check your payment records and recent messages. Then send a short email naming the invoice and the date they gave you, and ask whether payment was sent or when it will be sent. Leave room for a transfer that is still processing or an invoice problem.

Email to adapt

Subject: Following up on the payment date for invoice [number]

Hi [client name],

I'm following up on invoice [number], originally due on [original due date]. You mentioned that payment would be sent by [promised date]. I haven't been able to match it to my records yet.

Has payment been sent? If so, could you share the payment date and reference so I can check? If it has not been sent, please let me know the date you now expect to send it, or what is holding it up.

The outstanding balance in my records is [amount and currency]. Payment instructions are [link or location on invoice].

Thanks,
[your name]

Use the client’s actual wording and date; if they said only “soon,” do not invent a promised date. Remove the payment-instructions line if it adds nothing, and check the balance before stating it. Reply in the existing invoice thread so the agreement and your follow-up stay together.

Check, write, and send

  1. Verify the balance. Check your bank or payment provider, any remittance advice, partial payments and messages from the client or their accounts team. A payment marked as sent may still need matching.
  2. Find the commitment. Read the email or note from the call and record the exact promised date. Keep it separate from the invoice’s original due date.
  3. Ask one clear question. Request the payment date and reference if it was sent; otherwise ask for a revised date or the specific blocker. Include the invoice number so the recipient can locate it.
  4. Review before sending. Check the recipient, amount, both dates and payment route. Keep a copy of the reply and check your records again on the date the client gives you.

FAQ

What should I write when a client misses a promised payment date?

Refer to the invoice number and the date the client actually gave you. Say you cannot yet match the payment to your records, then ask whether it was sent or when it will be sent. Keep the tone factual while you verify what happened.

Should I demand payment again if the client says it was sent?

First ask for the payment date and reference, then compare them with your own records. If the payment is still processing or was applied to another invoice, that information helps you reconcile it without making an unsupported claim.

What if only part of the invoice was paid?

Acknowledge what you received and state the verified remaining balance. Ask when that balance will be sent. Do not copy the full original amount into a reminder as though nothing was paid.
