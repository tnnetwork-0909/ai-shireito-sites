How to Email Accounts Payable About an Unpaid Invoice

To email accounts payable about an unpaid invoice, use the payment-contact address your client gave you and include the invoice number, amount, currency and due date. Ask whether the invoice was received, approved or held for a specific reason, then request an expected payment date if it is ready to pay. Check your own records before saying that payment is late or missing.

Status-check email you can adapt

Subject: Payment status for invoice [number]

Hello [accounts payable contact or team],

I'm checking the status of invoice [number] for [amount and currency], due on [original due date]. I submitted it through [the client's required route] on [actual submission date].

Could you confirm that it is in your system and let me know whether it is approved, awaiting a document or correction, or scheduled for payment? If it is approved, could you share the expected payment date? If something is missing, please tell me exactly what you need and where to send it.

[If relevant: The purchase order reference on the invoice is [verified number].]
[If attached: I have attached a copy of the invoice for reference.]

Thank you,
[your name]

Replace every bracketed item and delete the optional lines that do not apply. Do not guess a purchase order number or say an invoice is attached unless you have attached it. If your client requires a supplier portal, follow that route for submitting or correcting an invoice; use the email to clarify its status.

Send a useful status request

  1. Check what you know. Compare the invoice, agreed due date, your submission record and payment records. If your client said payment was sent, reconcile that claim before calling the invoice unpaid.
  2. Find the correct contact. Use the accounts payable address or portal in the client's invoice instructions. If you only have a project contact, ask them which team handles payment and whether they need to introduce you.
  3. Make the invoice easy to locate. Put its number in the subject and give the amount, currency, due date and actual submission route. Add a confirmed purchase order or supplier reference only if the client uses one.
  4. Ask for a specific status. Request confirmation of receipt and whether the invoice is approved, held or scheduled. If it is held, ask for the exact missing item and correct submission route; if approved, ask for the expected payment date.
  5. Keep the record accurate. Send the message from your own email account, save the reply with the invoice, and correct or resubmit only through the client's stated process. Review the recipient and attachment before sending.

FAQ

What should I put in the subject line to accounts payable?

Include the exact invoice number and a clear purpose, such as “Payment status for invoice [number].” That gives the team a reliable way to find the record. Add a purchase order reference only if you have confirmed it.

Should I email accounts payable or my project contact?

Follow the client's stated invoice and payment-contact process. If there is a designated accounts payable inbox, use it for the status request. If you do not know the right route, ask your project contact rather than guessing an address.

What if accounts payable says the invoice is on hold?

Ask which document, reference or approval is missing and who can resolve it. Check the answer with your client contact, correct the invoice if needed, and resubmit through the required channel. Ask for confirmation that the corrected invoice was accepted.

Can NudgePay tell me whether the invoice was approved?

No. NudgePay uses the details you enter to show reminder drafts for different due-date stages and tones. Ask the client's accounts payable team for status, then edit a draft in your email app to reflect their answer.
