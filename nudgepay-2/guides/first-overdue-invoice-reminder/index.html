Your first overdue invoice reminder: what to write

When an invoice first becomes overdue, a short note can make the next step obvious. Verify the balance, copy the example, and replace the bracketed fields before you send it.

Email example

Subject: Invoice [invoice number] is overdue

Hi [client name],

Invoice [invoice number] for [amount and currency] was due on [due date] and is now overdue.

Payment details: [link or instructions]

Please arrange payment and let me know once it has been sent. If there is an issue with the invoice, please reply so I can help resolve it.

Thanks,
[your name]

Check the facts before pressing send

  • Check your records. Confirm that the due date has passed, the balance is still outstanding, and a recent transfer is not simply awaiting confirmation.
  • Use the remaining amount. If the client made a partial payment, state the actual balance rather than the original invoice total.
  • Keep the request simple. Ask for payment and make the method easy to find. Attach the invoice again if the client might need it.
  • Leave room for a real problem. A missing purchase order, wrong billing details, or a disputed line item needs a reply and correction, not a harsher message.

If the client replies

If they say payment was sent, thank them and ask for the transfer date or reference if you cannot match it yet. Confirm receipt only after you can verify it. If they identify an error, correct the invoice and agree on the next payment step. Keep the exchange in the same email thread so the details stay together.

Do not add a late fee, legal threat, or claim that you sent earlier reminders unless it is accurate and supported by your agreement. A first note is strongest when every fact can be checked.

FAQ

What should a first overdue invoice email say?

State the invoice number, outstanding balance and original due date, then ask for payment. Include the payment route or attach the invoice and invite the client to flag an error.

Should I mention a late fee in the first reminder?

Do not add a fee that is absent from your agreement. A first note can stay focused on the unpaid invoice and a clear way to resolve it.

What if the client says payment was sent?

Ask for the transfer date or reference if you cannot match it to your records. Confirm receipt only after you have verified it.

What if payment is still missing later?

Check the balance and any reply before sending another message. If the invoice is still open after two weeks, the 14-day follow-up guide shows a more direct email.
