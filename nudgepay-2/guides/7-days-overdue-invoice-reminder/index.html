An unanswered earlier reminder

7 Days Overdue Invoice Reminder Email for Freelancers

If a freelance invoice is seven days overdue and an earlier reminder got no reply, send a short follow-up that names the invoice, outstanding balance and original due date. Ask whether payment is in progress or something is blocking it, and include a way to find the invoice and pay. Mention the earlier reminder only if you sent one.

Email you can adapt

Subject: Follow-up on invoice [number], one week overdue

Hi [client name],

I'm following up on invoice [number] for [outstanding amount and currency], due [full due date]. My records still show the balance open. I sent a reminder on [actual date], but I may have missed an update from your team.

Could you check whether the invoice has reached the person who approves payment? If payment is in progress, please let me know the expected date. If a document or correction is needed, tell me what is missing and I'll address it.

Payment details: [link or location on the attached invoice]

Thanks,
[your name]

Remove the sentence about the earlier reminder if you did not send one. Replace every bracketed field, attach the invoice only if you say it is attached, and use the unpaid balance after any partial payment. This email asks for a status and a practical next step; it does not invent a fee or a consequence.

Send the follow-up in four steps

  1. Verify the timing and balance. Count seven calendar days from the invoice due date. Check payment records and any transfer notice before saying the balance is open.
  2. Read the earlier thread. Use the actual date of your first note. If the client already reported a missing purchase order, wrong billing name or other blocker, address that issue instead of repeating the request.
  3. Make the invoice easy to route. Put the invoice number in the subject, include the current balance and due date, and attach the invoice or point to its payment instructions. Ask who handles approval if your contact may not be that person.
  4. Ask one answerable question. Request the expected payment date or the specific item holding payment up. Send from your own email account and keep the reply with the invoice record.

Adjust the message to the reply you have

If the client answers only “soon,” the guide to asking for a specific payment date gives a narrower follow-up. If the invoice remains unpaid at two weeks, review the 14-day reminder after checking any new reply.

FAQ

What should I say when an invoice is a week overdue?

Name the invoice number, unpaid balance and original due date, then ask whether payment is in progress or an approval is missing. Give the client a clear route to the invoice and payment instructions. Keep the tone factual even if an earlier message went unanswered.

Is seven days late too soon for a second reminder?

Check your agreed terms, the first reminder and any reply before choosing the timing. A short follow-up can be useful when the balance remains open, but an issue already raised by the client needs a response first. Do not call it a second reminder if you have not sent a first one.

Should I copy the client’s accounts payable team?

If you already know the right payment contact and are allowed to use that address, sending the invoice to them may resolve a routing delay. If you do not know who approves payment, ask your client to identify the person. Keep the message limited to the invoice facts they need.

What if the client says they already paid?

Check your payment records before following up again. If you cannot match the transfer, ask for its date or reference and confirm receipt only after you verify it. Correct the outstanding balance in any later reminder.
