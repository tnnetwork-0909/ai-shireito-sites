When a balance remains

Partial Payment Reminder Email for Freelancers

After a client pays part of an invoice, confirm the payment in your records and calculate the remaining balance before writing. Thank them for the amount received, identify the invoice and unpaid balance, and ask when or how they will pay the rest. Use the agreed payment schedule if there is one.

Email to adapt

Subject: Remaining balance on invoice [number]

Hi [client name],

Thank you for your payment of [amount received] toward invoice [number]. I have recorded it against the invoice. The remaining balance is [verified balance].

The balance was due on [original due date]. Could you let me know when you expect to send the remainder? You can use [existing payment link or instructions]. If your records show a different balance, please reply with the details so we can compare them.

Thanks,
[your name]

Use the original due-date sentence only when it is still accurate. If you agreed to installments or a revised date, replace it with the actual agreement. If the payment has been reported but has not reached your records, do not thank the client for a received amount yet; ask for the transfer date and reference first.

Work out the balance before sending

  1. Check the invoice and payment record. Start with the current invoice total, including any corrections already agreed. Match each posted payment to the invoice number; a client's message that a transfer was sent is not proof that you received it.
  2. Subtract verified payments and agreed credits. The result is the remaining balance to state in your email. Check the currency and any payment fees or deductions against your own agreement and records rather than guessing.
  3. Check the due date. Look for an installment plan or revised date in the email thread. Do not describe the remainder as overdue if it is not due yet.
  4. Send the corrected message. Reply in the existing invoice thread if practical, include the payment route, and attach or link the current invoice only if it helps the client reconcile the balance. Review the recipient and every figure before sending.

If the client agrees that the remaining balance is due but gives no date, the guide on asking for a payment date has a focused follow-up. For an invoice with no payment recorded, start with the first overdue reminder instead.

FAQ

How do I write a partial payment reminder email?

Thank the client for the payment you have verified, give the invoice number and remaining balance, and make one clear request about the rest. Include the agreed payment method or a link to the current invoice. Check the due date before calling the balance late.

Should I mention the original invoice total?

You can include it when it helps explain the calculation, along with the posted payment and any agreed credit. Make the remaining balance the amount you ask the client to pay. Check that the total still matches the current invoice.

What if the client has an installment plan?

Refer to the agreed installment and its due date rather than asking for the whole remainder early. A partial payment can be fully on schedule. Change the example email so it accurately reflects what both sides agreed.

Can NudgePay calculate the unpaid balance or send the email?

No. Check payments and calculate the balance in your own records, then edit a copied NudgePay draft in your email composer. NudgePay does not track payments or send messages for you.
