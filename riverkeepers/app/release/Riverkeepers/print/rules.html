Riverkeepers

A solo map game about a river that remembers. Build a valley in the Past, then draw its roads in the Future. Your settlements, levees, bridges, and flood damage become the map you must live with.

You need: the Past Sheet, the Future Sheet, a pencil, and two six-sided dice. A game takes about 20–30 minutes. If you have no dice, write 1–6 on six equal paper slips. Fold and mix them, draw one number, then replace and mix before every roll. Draw twice for a two-die roll.

Read the map

Both sheets have columns A–G and rows 1–6. Column D is the river. C and E are the riverside cells. Cells touch only along a side, never at a corner. For example, C3 touches B3, C2, C4, and D3.

Write symbols in the cells. Keep the dice, events, and choices in the turn log if you want to replay your game. You may erase planning marks; only the final marks matter.

Past: The Founding (8 turns)

On each turn:

  1. Roll two dice. Choose one as your Row (1–6); the other is Water. Doubles give you no choice.
  2. Build exactly one legal thing in the chosen row. If neither die can be assigned to a row with any legal build, build nothing and mark 1 Hardship. In that case, use the lower die as Water.
  3. If you rolled doubles, roll one die on the Past Event table and resolve it now.
  4. Add the Water die to the Water Track, with any event change. Each flood mark you reach or pass causes one Flood. Resolve each Flood separately.
BuildSymbolWhere it may go
SettlementSAny empty land cell (any column except D) outside a Flood Zone. It may not share a side with another Settlement.
LeveeLAn empty riverside cell (C or E) outside a Flood Zone.
BridgeBThe empty river cell D of the chosen row, if C or E of that row already has a Settlement (flooded or dry) or Levee. You may have at most 3 Bridges.

“Empty” means no building is written there. A flooded Settlement remains on the sheet and still blocks adjacent Settlement placement. A ruined Bridge is still occupied; it cannot be built again.

Past Events

Roll once on doubles, after your usual build. If an event grants a build and none is legal, ignore that part of the event.

d6EventDo this
1Good HarvestBuild one extra Settlement in any row, following normal placement rules.
2Spring ThawAdd 3 extra Water this turn.
3The FerrymanBuild one extra Bridge in any eligible row, within the 3 Bridge limit.
4Dry SummerAdd no Water from this turn's Water die.
5Founders' FeudMark 1 Hardship.
6Great StormFlood now: roll for its row. Then continue with normal Water.

Floods

The Water Track starts at 0 and has flood marks at 9, 18, 27, and 36. Crossed marks are used once each. Stop the track at 40 if Water goes higher.

For each Flood, roll one die for its row. Shade C and E in that row as Flood Zones, except a cell holding a Levee. A Settlement there becomes flooded; keep its S and shade its cell. A Bridge in D of that row becomes ruined unless either C or E of that row has a Levee. Mark a ruined Bridge B×. A Levee protects its own cell and that row's Bridge, but not the opposite bank. You cannot build on a shaded Flood Zone later. If the same row floods again, apply the same rule to its current marks.

Future: The Keepers (8 turns)

Your network consists of all Towns T, roads r, Embankments =, Old Bridges OB, and New Bridges NB, even if those marks are in separate groups. Roads connect along cell sides. Open river ~ cannot be entered or crossed. A Bridge links only sideways, to C and E of its own row; Bridges in neighboring rows of column D never connect to each other. A Bridge joins the two banks only when network cells sit on both C and E of its row.

On each turn:

  1. Roll two dice. If they match, roll once on the Future Event table and resolve the event before drawing your usual road.
  2. Choose one die as your Row. The other gives Steps: 1–3 = 1 Step; 4–5 = 2 Steps; 6 = 3 Steps. Doubles give you no choice. Apply any event that changes Steps.
  3. Draw one or more new road cells, spending no more than your Steps in total. Every new cell must touch a network cell by a side; a cell you drew earlier this turn counts. At least one new cell must be in your chosen Row. You may leave unused Steps.
  4. If no legal road is possible with either assignment, draw nothing and mark 1 Hardship. If a legal road is possible, you must draw one.

Future Events

Roll once on doubles, before assigning your dice. If an event has no legal place to act, ignore that part and continue the turn. Free cells do not use Steps or satisfy the chosen Row requirement for your usual road.

d6EventDo this
1Traveling MasonMark NB on one open river cell ~ whose C or E cell in the same row is part of your network.
2Late FloodRoll a row. Plain road r on C and E of that row becomes Marsh M, unless the row has an Embankment =.
3Old MapGain 1 Relic.
4Fair WeatherAdd 2 Steps to this turn's usual road.
5FogThis turn's usual road has only 1 Step.
6Founders' RoadDraw one free road cell on land next to any Town. It may enter blank land, Ruins, or Marsh; gain a Relic if it enters Ruins.

A Late Flood can break a network. The flooded road is now Marsh, and you may later spend 2 Steps to enter it again.

Score and ending

After Future turn 8, trace each connected group of network cells. Choose the group worth the most points from 3 per Town plus 5 if it reaches both banks through a bridge (a group reaches both banks only through a Bridge whose C and E cells are both in the group). This is your Main Network. Groups with no Town cannot be the Main Network. Other Towns do not score. Then calculate:

Score = 3 × Towns in Main Network + 5 if Main Network reaches both banks + 2 × Relics − total Hardship from both eras.

Check endings from top to bottom; use the first that fits:

  1. The Drowned Valley: no Town survived the Past; score 0.
  2. The River United: Main Network reaches both banks and contains at least 5 Towns.
  3. The Long Bridge: Main Network reaches both banks and contains fewer than 5 Towns.
  4. Two Shores: Main Network does not reach both banks and contains at least 3 Towns.
  5. Scattered Hamlets: any other result.
