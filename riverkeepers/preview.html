One choice. Two futures.

This self-contained scene answers one question: does a Past build change the way you cross the river later?

1. Look at the river row

This is a small part of a Past map. A settlement sits at C3, and its bridge stands at D3. Another settlement sits at B2. The rest of the map is omitted. You may add exactly one legal build in row 3:

       B   C   D   E   F
row 2  S   .   ~   .   .
row 3  .   S   B   .   .

Choose A: put a Levee at E3. Choose B: put a Settlement at F3. Both are legal under the full rules.

2. The same flood hits either choice

The Water Track crosses a flood mark and the flood die names row 3. Floods shade the riverside cells C and E, except a cell holding a Levee. A Levee also protects that row's Bridge. The F3 Settlement is outside the riverside flood cells.

In the Future, dry Settlements become Towns; flooded Settlements become Ruins; a Levee becomes an Embankment; a sound Bridge becomes an Old Bridge; a broken Bridge costs two road Steps to repair. The next road turn gives you three Steps and requires a new cell in row 3. Start from the Town at B2.

3. Reveal your result

A · You built the Levee at E3

C3 floods and becomes Ruins. E3 stays dry and becomes an Embankment. D3 survives as an Old Bridge. Spend one Step to draw B3 and one Step to enter C3; that also gives you one Relic. The route B2 → B3 → C3 → D3 → E3 now reaches both banks. One Step remains unused.

B · You built the Settlement at F3

C3 floods and becomes Ruins. E3 becomes Marsh, D3 becomes a Broken Bridge, and F3 becomes a Town. Spend one Step to draw B3 and one Step to enter C3; that gives you one Relic. Only one Step remains, so you cannot pay the two Steps needed to repair D3 this turn. Your western route cannot cross yet, and the new Town at F3 is separate from it.

This is a fixed, cropped example of one Past choice and one Future road turn. It is complete as a demonstration, but it does not include the full event table, eight turns in each era, scoring, or endings.
