Roll and write vs flip and write: the difference

A roll and write uses dice to generate a result you record or use on a sheet; a flip and write uses cards you turn over. Both make you decide what to mark, so check the actual turn rules and components before choosing a game, especially if you want to avoid dice or a deck.

What changes when you roll or flip?

Compare the input, then read each game's rules
CheckRoll and writeFlip and write
Source of the resultOne or more dice are rolled.One or more cards are revealed.
What you bringUsually dice, a sheet, and a writing tool.Usually a deck, a sheet, and a writing tool.
Repeated resultsFresh rolls can repeat a number immediately.Card frequency and reshuffling depend on that game's deck rules.
What to check in a downloadDie types and whether they are supplied or must be brought.Whether cards are supplied, need printing or cutting, and when to reshuffle.

The writing can mean filling boxes, placing numbers, or drawing a route. A game may combine dice and cards, so its own component list and first-turn instructions are more reliable than a broad genre label. For a published card-driven example, the Welcome to the Moon rules describe flipping cards to supply numbers and actions for an Adventure sheet.

Choose by the turn you want to take

  1. Read the component list. If you already have dice, a dice-driven sheet game may be quick to set up. If you prefer cards, check whether the download includes a ready-to-use deck or cards you must make.
  2. Read the first turn. Find the instruction that creates a result: “roll” points to dice, while “flip” or “reveal” points to cards. Note whether you may choose among results or must use one assigned result.
  3. Check the sheet. Locate where the result is written and what placement restrictions apply. The interesting choice is often where or how to use the result, not merely generating it.
  4. Check how play ends. Look for a fixed number of rounds, a filled sheet, an objective, or scoring instructions. Neither input method determines the game's length or difficulty by itself.

If you want a game without card assembly, check its complete component list; “flip and write” does not tell you how the deck is delivered. The no-cutting guide shows how to inspect downloadable components.

An actual two-dice turn in Riverkeepers

Riverkeepers uses a Past map sheet with rows 1–6 and a river in column D. At the start of a Past turn, roll two six-sided dice. Choose one result as the Row where you build, and use the other as Water; doubles remove that assignment choice and trigger a Past Event after the normal build.

On a blank map, a roll of 3 and 5 lets you choose row 3 and put a Settlement at C3, then add 5 Water. You could instead build legally in row 5 and add 3 Water. C3 is land beside the river, while D3 is river and cannot hold a Settlement. In the Future, the two dice become a Row and a number of road Steps, so the written map itself changes what you can do next.

FAQ

Is a flip and write still a kind of roll and write?

People often group these sheet-marking games together. The useful distinction is the source of each turn's input: dice are rolled in a roll and write, while cards are turned over in a flip and write. Always follow the specific game's rules.

Are flip and write games less random?

Not automatically. Cards may come from a shuffled deck, and dice produce uncertain rolls. How much planning is possible depends on the deck or dice rules and on the choices the game gives you after a result appears.

Do I need to print a new sheet every game?

That depends on the game and whether its sheet can be erased or reprinted. Riverkeepers uses two printable write-on pages for a full game, and you may print fresh copies for your own play. The sheet reuse guide compares practical reset methods.

Does Riverkeepers use cards or dice?

The complete Riverkeepers paper game uses two ordinary six-sided dice, a pencil, and two sheets; it does not use cards. Its free browser preview is a fixed scene that asks you to choose between two builds, with no roll required in that preview.
