How to reuse roll and write sheets

Keep an unmarked copy of each game sheet, then either print a fresh copy for each play or place a copy in a clear writable sleeve and erase the sleeve after scoring. Check the game's rules and test your marker on the sleeve first; erase only after you have recorded anything you want to keep.

A sleeve or laminate changes the writing surface, not the game's rules. If a game asks you to cross out, shade, and later change a mark, make sure you can still tell the current state from an earlier one. Keep the original download or clean paper copy so the blank layout is never lost.

A practical reset routine

  1. Save the blank sheets. Keep the original game files together. For a paper master, write on a separate copy rather than the last clean one.
  2. Check the whole sheet. Locate the map, turn log, event table, and score fields. A reusable method has to leave every field visible and writable.
  3. Test a corner of the sleeve. Write a small mark, wait, rub it lightly, and erase it using the sleeve maker's directions. Test before covering a whole map.
  4. Play and score before clearing. Keep turn logs and map changes readable until the final score is settled. Copy the score or photograph your own finished sheet if you want a record.
  5. Reset both sides of the game. Wipe each sleeve and check that no old shading or marks remain before starting again.

If an erasable marker smears or a sleeve hides small symbols, use a fresh paper copy instead. The cheapest setup is not useful when it makes the map hard to read.

Keep both Riverkeepers maps intact

Riverkeepers has a Past sheet and a Future sheet, each with a map, turn log, and event details. During Past play you mark Settlements S, Levees L, Bridges B, and Flood Zones. After Past turn 8, you copy every map cell to the Future sheet: for example, a dry S becomes a Town T, while a flooded S becomes Ruins R.

Keep the Past sheet visible until the full transfer is complete, including its Hardship total. Then use the Future sheet for roads, Relics, scoring, and the ending. Do not wipe either sheet midgame: a ruined bridge, marsh, or shaded bank can affect later decisions. At the end, record the final score and ending before erasing reusable surfaces.

FAQ

Can I put a roll-and-write sheet in a plastic sleeve?

Yes, if the sheet remains easy to read and the sleeve works with your chosen erasable marker. Test a small mark for smudging and clean removal before playing. The pen should touch the sleeve, not the paper inside it.

Should I laminate a game sheet or print a new one?

Print a new copy when you want to keep a finished game or make pencil corrections. A laminated copy can suit repeated plays, but test whether its surface lets you change and erase all of the game's marks. Check the game's personal-copy terms before reprinting.

Can I erase Riverkeepers' Past map after turn eight?

Wait until you have copied every cell and the Past Hardship total to the Future sheet. Keeping the Past map beside you also makes it easier to check a conversion. Once the game is scored and you have saved any record you want, you can reset a reusable sleeve.

Do I need to reprint the rules every time?

No. Riverkeepers' rules and complete example can stay open in the downloaded HTML files while you use fresh play sheets. Other games vary, so check whether rules or tables are printed on their write-on sheets.
