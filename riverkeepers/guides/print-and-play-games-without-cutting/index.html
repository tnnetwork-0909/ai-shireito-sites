Print and play games without cutting

A quick check before you download

  1. Find the play surface. Look for a map, score sheet, or board you can mark directly. A page of card fronts means cutting may be part of setup.
  2. Read the full component list. Check for dice, pencils, counters, card sleeves, or a separate deck. “No cutting” does not mean “no other supplies.”
  3. Check the paper and file type. Confirm that the game supports your paper size and whether the download is a PDF or a file opened in a browser.
  4. Check what must be printed again. Some games need a fresh play sheet for every session; rules can often stay on screen.

A two-sheet example: Riverkeepers

Riverkeepers uses a Past map sheet and a Future map sheet. You write settlements, levees, and bridges on the first; after eight Past turns, you copy the resulting cells onto the second and draw roads. The sheets are black and white, and there are no cards, counters, or pieces to cut. You supply a pencil and two six-sided dice; the rules also explain a paper-slip substitute for missing dice.

The full download is a ZIP of HTML and CSS files, not a PDF. After opening index.html, the print-ready sheets open in a browser; complete rules and a worked game are included. See the contents and purchase details.

Print the sheets without losing a map edge

  1. Open the print-ready sheets from the downloaded game's start page.
  2. Set A4 or US Letter paper, black and white, one-sided, and 100% scale. Turn off browser headers and footers.
  3. Check the print preview for both pages: Past first, Future second. Make sure the outer map columns and turn logs are visible.
  4. If your printer clips an edge, use its “fit to printable area” setting and check the preview again. Keep the rules on screen or print them separately.

FAQ

Do all print and play games require cutting?

No. Games played by writing on a sheet can go straight from printer to table. Card and token kits often need cutting, so check the actual component list before downloading.

Does “no cutting” mean I only need paper?

No. A sheet game may still require a pencil, dice, or other pieces. Riverkeepers needs a pencil and two six-sided dice unless you use the paper-slip substitute described in its rules.

Can I use ordinary black-and-white printer paper?

For Riverkeepers, yes: its two play sheets are designed for black-and-white printing on A4 or US Letter. Other games may rely on color or thicker paper, so check their own instructions.

Should I print at 100% or fit to page?

Start Riverkeepers at 100% scale with headers and footers off. If an edge is clipped by your printer, switch to “fit to printable area” and inspect the preview before playing.
