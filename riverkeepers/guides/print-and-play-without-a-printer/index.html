Print and play without a printer

You can play some print-and-play games without a printer by copying a simple write-on sheet onto blank paper and keeping the rules open on a screen. Check the components first: a grid and score track are practical to redraw, while a deck of detailed cards or a board that depends on artwork may need a print shop or an official digital version.

If the files are only printable sheets, opening them in a browser does not automatically make them interactive. Avoid writing over a screenshot unless the game explicitly supports that method and you have a way to save your marks.

How to copy a write-on game sheet

  1. Inspect the whole sheet and rules. List the map or play area, turn record, tracks, scoring boxes, and any tables you must consult. Decide what can stay visible on your screen.
  2. Draw the play area first. Match the number and labels of rows and columns. Give each space room for the game's marks, and copy any special spaces before play.
  3. Add the changing records. Make room for each turn, the resources or hazards that change, and the final score. Copy threshold numbers exactly; an omitted threshold can change a game.
  4. Check one sample move against the rules. Find a location on your copy, record a mock result, and confirm that the labels line up with the original sheet. Erase the mock marks before starting.
  5. Play with the rules available. Keep the original file open for event wording and edge cases. If you cannot copy an essential component accurately, use a printed copy instead.

This method is a setup workaround, not a promise that every print-and-play game is designed for hand copying.

A concrete example with Riverkeepers

The complete Riverkeepers download is a ZIP of HTML and CSS. After unzipping it, open index.html and choose Open the play sheets. Those two pages are meant to be printed, but their simple labeled maps can also be copied onto blank paper while the rules and event tables stay on screen.

  1. On one page, draw the Past map with columns A–G and rows 1–6. Mark column D as river and C and E as its banks. Leave room to write settlements (S), levees (L), bridges (B), and flood shading.
  2. Beside it, draw an eight-turn log and a Water total starting at 0. Mark flood thresholds at 9, 18, 27, and 36; leave space for Hardship and Bridges built. Keep the Past Event table visible in the rules or sheet file.
  3. On a second page, draw the same labeled map for the Future. Leave space for an eight-turn log, Relics, Past and Future Hardship, the Main Network's Town count, crossing bonus, final score, and ending. Keep the Future Event table and conversion guide visible.
  4. After Past turn 8, copy every cell to the Future map using the game's conversion guide, then play the Future turns. You still need a pencil and two six-sided dice, or the numbered-slip substitute in the rules.

For a check before copying the entire map, C3 is a riverside land cell in row 3, while D3 is river. A settlement belongs on legal land, not in D3. The two maps and turn records must stay separate so the Past marks can become the Future starting position.

FAQ

Can every print-and-play game be played without printing?

No. A small write-on grid may be easy to copy, but detailed cards, hidden information, or boards that rely on precise graphics can be difficult to reproduce. Check the actual components and rules before deciding.

Can I play Riverkeepers entirely in its browser preview?

No. The free preview presents one fixed decision and its consequence. The full game uses two play sheets for eight Past turns and, if a Town survives, eight Future turns.

What must I copy from the Riverkeepers sheets?

Copy both labeled maps and the spaces where you will record turns, Water, Hardship, Relics, and final scoring. You can read the event tables and full rules on screen, but the map labels and flood thresholds on paper must match the originals.

Do I need dice if I copy the sheets by hand?

Yes, the complete game still needs two six-sided results per turn. Its rules explain how to draw from six numbered paper slips, replacing and mixing after each draw, if you have no dice.
